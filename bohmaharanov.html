The Aharonov-Bohm Effect

Published on October 7, 2026

Introduction: The Aharonov-Bohm effect is one of those bizare quantum phenomena that really makes you question everything you thing you know about physics. In short the Aharonov-Bohm effect is a phenomenon in which a magnetic vector potential produces measurable changes to physical system. This challenges our classical understanding of potentials as mear mathematical fiction and electromagnetic fields as the "real" physics.

1. Derivation

Let \( \psi (x) \) describe an electron and \(B\) a magnetic field such that for \(B(x)\neq 0\) we have \(\psi (x)=0 \). For the region in which the wavefunction is non zero we therfore have \(B=\nabla\times A=0\). Thus it follows from the Helmholz-Theorem that there exists a scalar field \( \chi\) such that \(A=\nabla \chi\). Now choose a \(x_0\) and set \(\chi (x_0)=0\). Let \( \gamma\) be a curve with \(\gamma (0)=x_0\) and \(\gamma (1)=x \). From the gradient theorem follows $$\chi(x)=\int_{\gamma} dx \dot A .$$
