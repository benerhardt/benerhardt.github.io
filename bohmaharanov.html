The Aharonov-Bohm Effect

Published on October 7, 2026

Introduction: The Aharonov-Bohm effect is one of those bizare quantum phenomena that really makes you question everything you thing you know about physics. In short the Aharonov-Bohm effect is a phenomenon in which a magnetic vector potential produces measurable changes to physical system. This challenges our classical understanding of potentials as mear mathematical fiction and electromagnetic fields as the "real" physics.

1. Derivation

Let \( \psi (x) \) describe an electron and \(B\) a magnetic field such that for \(B(x)\neq 0\) we have \(\psi (x)=0 \). For the region in which the wavefunction is non zero we therfore have \(B=\nabla\times A=0\). Thus it follows from the Helmholz-Theorem that there exists a scalar field \( \chi\) such that \(A=\nabla \chi\). Now choose a \(x_0\) and set \(\chi (x_0)=0\). Let \( \gamma\) be a curve with \(\gamma (0)=x_0\) and \(\gamma (1)=x \). From the gradient theorem follows $$\chi(x)=\int_{\gamma} dx \cdot A .$$ We now apply the following gauge-transformation: \( A\rightarrow A'=A-\nabla \chi=0\) and \(\psi\rightarrow \psi '= \text{exp} \left(\frac{ie}{\hbar}\chi\right)\psi\). Now consider two paths \(\gamma_1\) and \(\gamma_2\) from \(x_0\) to \(x\). This might be thought of as the paths taken by the elctron after opening either slit one or two, whilst leaving the other slit closed, in the double-slit experiment. These two paths corrospond to two wave functions \(\psi_1\) and \(\psi_2\). $$\psi_j(x)=\text{exp}\left(-\frac{ie}{\hbar}\int_{\gamma_1}dx\cdot A\right)\psi_j'(x)$$ Notice that in theo prime gauge, the wavefunction isn't affected by the vector potential at all. This is simply the wavefunktion, if not magnetic field is at all present. Now suppose both paths are possible, as if we were to open both slits. Now the wavefunctions interfere with eachother. Mathematically this means we add them together, which yields $$\psi_1+\psi_2=\text{exp}\left(-\frac{ie}{\hbar}\int_{\gamma_1} dx \cdot A\right)\left[\psi_1'+\text{exp}\left(\frac{ie}{\hbar}\int_{\gamma_1}dx\cdot A-\frac{ie}{\hbar} \int_{\gamma_2}dx\cdot A\right)\psi_2'\right]$$

. Consider the Term in the second exponent. Thes two path integrals form a single loop integral. Let \(\gamma=\gamma_1+(-\gamma_2)\) and \(S\) such that \(\text{im}(\gamma)=\partial S\). Then $$\int_{\gamma_1} dx\cdot A-\int_{\gamma_2}dx\cdot A=\oint_{\gamma}dx\cdot A.$$ Then by Stokes' theorem we have $$\oint_{\gamma}dx\cdot A=\int_{S}dS\cdot B.$$
